Five old methods of reading a day, in one bilingual app.

KKORI brings together Liuyao coin divination, Plum Blossom Numerology, Qimen Dunjia compass readings, and a daily almanac — plus a separate cycle tracker — built for readers of Traditional Chinese and Korean.

KKORI — an original character by Makgolli Studio

Four ways to ask

Each method has its own history and its own way of framing a question. KKORI keeps them separate, rather than flattening them into one generic "fortune" feature.

Liuyao

六爻

Coin-toss divination, building a hexagram one throw at a time.

Plum Blossom Numerology

梅花易數

Reading a moment through number, time, and circumstance.

Qimen Dunjia

奇門遁甲

A compass reading across the eight directions and 24 mountains.

Daily Almanac

流日

What a given day favors and what it doesn't, at a glance.

Also inside

Not every part of KKORI is a reading. Alongside the four divination methods, the app includes a couple of practical, everyday tools — a calendar and a reminder list, not fortune-telling features.

Cycle Tracker

A simple calendar for tracking and editing your own cycle.

Reminders

Daily routine alerts — medication, chores, anything on a schedule.

Built bilingual

Every reading, term, and result is written natively for each language, not machine-translated from one into the other.

繁體中文

Traditional Chinese, for readers in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau.

한국어

Korean, with its own terminology for each method of reading.
